# 0017 — 3D scenes, stereo and VR: own splat renderer, WebXR without dependencies

**Context.** Phase 9: WebXR VR with gaze and controller activation and a VR-friendly
button subset; stereo panoramas; GLB scenes with 3D-placed hotspots and routes; Gaussian
splat scenes (lazy-loaded, with a justified library choice). Everything offline, under
the strict CSP (`script-src 'self'`, no `eval`, no blob or data-URL workers, no
WebAssembly). Done when the demo works in a WebXR emulator and a sample GLB and splat
scene show equipment hotspots.

**Decision.**
- **One scene model for 3D.** A GLB or splat scene has a `viewpoint` (model metres, Y
  up). The camera stays at the origin and the model is offset by −viewpoint, so a 3D scene
  turns like a panorama on screen and every existing feature (hotspots, signs, radar,
  sounds, training) keeps working from directions. Hotspots and equipment placements may
  be `world` points; the Viewer converts them to directions from the viewpoint. Routes
  gain `paths3d` (authored polylines on the model floor, per scene); chevrons follow them.
- **Stereo panoramas** (top-bottom or side-by-side) render each eye on its own three.js
  layer (1 = left, 2 = right); the flat screen shows the left eye, WebXR gives each eye
  its own image.
- **GLB** through three's `GLTFLoader` in a lazy chunk. Draco and meshopt compression are
  refused (their decoders need WebAssembly or workers); authors export uncompressed GLB.
- **Gaussian splats: our own renderer** (`packages/viewer/src/panorama/splat.ts`, lazy
  chunk, ~8 KB). Candidates checked against the CSP:
  - `@mkkellogg/gaussian-splats-3d` 0.4.7 — sorts in a Web Worker created from a blob URL,
    with a WebAssembly sorter: both blocked by our CSP, and loosening the CSP for the
    whole Viewer is not acceptable.
  - `@sparkjsdev/spark` 2.3.1 — WebAssembly core and data-URL workers: same problem.
  Ours: instanced quads, the standard EWA projection of each 3D covariance to a screen
  ellipse, per-splat data in a float texture, back-to-front order from a CPU counting sort
  on 16-bit depth keys, re-sorted only when the view turns more than ~1.5°. Formats:
  `.splat` and binary `.ply` (3DGS, degree-0 colour); `.ksplat` and `.spz` are converted
  first. Practical up to a few hundred thousand splats; larger captures are decimated at
  import (Phase 12 can move sorting off the main thread if a CSP-clean way is needed).
- **WebXR VR without new runtime dependencies** (`packages/viewer/src/vr.ts`, lazy chunk
  loaded after the session is granted). The VR button appears only where
  `navigator.xr.isSessionSupported('immersive-vr')` says yes. In the headset:
  - the scene's hotspots and equipment signs as cards drawn from the same kit artwork as
    the screen (signs, markers, buttons, labels), at a constant angular size;
  - route chevrons lying on the floor;
  - the VR-friendly toolbar: home, previous, next, evacuation route, exit VR, below the
    line of sight where the reader looked on arrival (re-placed on each scene);
  - an open dialog (information, equipment details, a question) mirrored as a panel with
    its text and its buttons; while it is open only its buttons respond (it is modal on
    screen too);
  - selection by controller ray and trigger, or — with no tracked controllers — by gaze:
    a reticle whose ring fills over 1.5 s (`vrDwellMs`; 0 turns it off). A target must be
    looked away from before it can be selected again.
  - Panoramas are 3 degrees of freedom (kept centred on the head); 3D scenes are 6 degrees
    of freedom around the viewpoint (lean and step), with no free locomotion: moving is
    by hotspots, as on screen.
- **Acceptance in an emulator.** IWER (Meta's Immersive Web Emulation Runtime, MIT) is a
  test-only dev dependency, bundled into one init script for Playwright; it emulates a
  Meta Quest 3 (stereo, two controllers). Tests enter VR from the skin, select by
  controller and by gaze, open and close a panel, toggle the route, change scene and exit;
  GLB and splat scenes show their equipment signs on screen and in VR.
- **Studio** places hotspots and equipment on the model surface in GLB and splat scenes
  (the Viewer's `pick` under the click; dragging moves over the surface).

**Consequences.** No new runtime dependency and no CSP change; the core bundle grows by
the XR hooks only (VR, GLB and splats are lazy chunks). Real-headset checks (Quest
browser, PC VR in a browser) are still to be done by Evactis; the desktop apps (WebView2)
show the VR button only if the webview reports `immersive-vr` support, so the expected VR
path is a headset's own browser opening a delivered package.
A Studio tool for drawing `paths3d` is not built yet (demo data only).
